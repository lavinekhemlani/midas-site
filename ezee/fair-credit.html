Why salary-backed credit costs less

The ezee team · 5 min read

Ask any Nigerian who has used a loan app and you'll hear the same story: a small amount, a short deadline, charges that pile up the moment you're late, and then the calls. Calls to you. Calls to your sister. Calls to your boss.

That model exists for a reason. A lender who doesn't know whether you'll repay has to price for the people who won't. So everyone pays for the defaults of a few, and the lender chases hard to recover what it can.

The salary changes everything

About five to six million Nigerians earn a regular salary, in the civil service, in banks, in oil and gas and in established companies. For them, there is a better way. When repayment is deducted from payroll on payday, the lender no longer has to guess whether the money will come back.

Less risk for the lender should mean a lower price for the borrower. That's the whole idea behind ezee loans.

Lower risk means the lender needs a smaller margin for losses. It means no army of collectors. And it means bigger amounts over longer terms, so a teacher can pay school fees in instalments instead of borrowing ₦30,000 at a time.

What we promise in return

  • One monthly amount, with every charge shown before you accept.
  • Repayments kept at or below a third of your take-home pay.
  • No calls or messages to anyone in your phone. Ever.

Fair credit isn't charity. It's just credit priced on what people really earn. That's what we're building.
