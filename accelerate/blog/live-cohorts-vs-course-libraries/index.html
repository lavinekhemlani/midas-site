Why live cohorts beat subscription course libraries for actually finishing

A $20/month library with a thousand videos sounds like a better deal than an 8-session cohort with a fixed price and a start date. It almost never produces a better outcome — and the reason is structural, not about content quality.

Infinite content has no finish line

A course library's business model rewards keeping you subscribed, not getting you to a finished outcome by a specific date. There's always another module, which means there's never a deadline. Most people who join one watch the first few videos with real intent, then quietly stop — not because the content was bad, but because nothing in the format ever forced the next step.

A live cohort has a start date, an end date, and other people watching

Three structural things change when a cohort is live, scheduled, and capped:

  • A real date you committed to — not "whenever I get to it."
  • Other people in the room, live, right now — social pressure that a pre-recorded video can't replicate, no matter how good it is.
  • A real cap on seats — the instructor can actually track whether you're keeping up, because there are 24 people, not 24,000.

None of this is about the live sessions being better-produced than recorded ones. It's that the format itself creates the conditions for finishing, which is the actual bottleneck for almost everyone who's tried to learn a skill on their own from a library of videos.

Replays exist — they're the backup, not the product

This doesn't mean recordings are bad. Every session is recorded and yours to keep. But the core product is the live session and the cohort around it; the replay is there so a missed week doesn't sink you, not so you can "watch it whenever" as the default plan.
