n8n vs Zapier vs Make: which one should you actually learn first

All three will let you connect apps and move data between them without writing a backend. They're not interchangeable, though — each one is genuinely better suited to a different kind of problem, and picking the wrong one first is the most common reason people bounce off automation entirely.

Zapier: broadest reach, highest cost per task

Zapier's real advantage is integration coverage — thousands of apps, most of them one click away. If the tool you need to connect is niche, Zapier has probably already built it. The tradeoff is pricing: you pay per "task" (roughly, per step executed), so a workflow that runs often or touches a lot of steps gets expensive fast. Zapier is the right first stop if you're non-technical, your tools are mainstream SaaS, and you're automating something that runs occasionally, not continuously.

Make: the visual middle ground

Make (formerly Integromat) trades some of Zapier's app coverage for a genuinely visual, flowchart-style builder and meaningfully cheaper pricing at volume. It's a better fit once your workflow has branches, loops, or error-handling logic you actually want to see laid out, not buried in a linear step list. Most people who outgrow Zapier's cost structure land here next.

n8n: the power-user's tool, and increasingly the AI-native one

n8n is open-source, self-hostable, and built with a node-based canvas that goes deeper than Make's — you can drop into actual JavaScript inside a node when the no-code building blocks run out. It's also where most of the current AI-agent tooling has landed first: n8n now ships dozens of AI-specific nodes, which makes it the natural home for workflows that call a model mid-process rather than just moving data between apps. The cost is a steeper learning curve, and self-hosting means you're responsible for keeping it running.

So which one first?

  • You're automating a handful of mainstream apps, occasionally, and don't want to think about servers: start with Zapier.
  • Your workflow has real branching logic and you're starting to feel the cost of per-task pricing: move to Make.
  • You want AI models inside the workflow itself — not just connecting apps, but agents making decisions mid-process: go straight to n8n.
